# Proof Pack

## Who it's for

Anyone who has to prove something went wrong: a scratched car, a parcel that never arrived, a laptop that died under warranty, a landlord keeping your deposit, or water damage at home. You probably already have the proof on your NAS. It's just spread across camera events, photos backed up from your phone and scanned documents.

## What it does

Tell it what happened and roughly when. Proof Pack then:

1. looks through your camera events, photos and documents for that time
2. puts everything it finds on one timeline, with the file each line came from
3. checks each thing an insurer, landlord or seller usually asks for, and marks it Verified, Single source, Conflict, Weak or Missing
4. shows you what changed if a document has more than one version, such as a revised repair quote
5. points out photos showing people your NAS has already grouped, so you can leave them out before sharing (it's still worth looking through them yourself)
6. after you say yes, copies the evidence into a new folder, makes an album and saves a summary note

It never moves, deletes or shares your files, and it doesn't decide who is right. If two sources disagree, you see both.

## How to use it

Just say what happened, for example:

- "Build a claim pack for the dent on my car from last Tuesday night."
- "My parcel went missing on 3 November. Pull together everything I have."
- "Get my deposit evidence ready. I moved in March 2025 and moved out last week."

When you have more proof later, say "Update my Proof Pack for the car claim." It re-checks only the items that aren't yet Verified, and makes a new album and note so your earlier ones stay as they were.

## Privacy

Proof Pack doesn't send or share your files, photos or camera events with anyone. It uses the AI set up on your NAS. Proof Pack can't see which AI that is, so it asks before reading your documents: if you've set Uliya to use a cloud AI service, text from those documents may be processed there.

## Good to know

Proof Pack organizes your own files. It is not legal or insurance advice. It works best when your photos have correct dates and your camera recordings are still stored. If some photos have no date, tell it which folder they're in.
